OUR STORIES / 02

Nagase
Yasuo.

Shinjuku, Tokyo · 63 years old

My name is Nagase Yasuo.

I’m from Mitaka, Tokyo. It’s about a 3-hour walk from Shinjuku. My older brother still lives there but we don’t see each other.

I've been on the streets of Shinjuku for 30 years. I'm 63 now so I’ve been homeless for around 30 years.

With the other homeless we never talk with each other about how or why we became homeless so I don’t know about other people’s stories but I can share my own. I left home and I was wondering what kind of place the streets would be like. I wanted to try it once. I didn’t like the idea of a job where I'm tied down too much, with too many rules to abide by. This is why, and it's kind of embarrassing to say, I wanted to try living on the streets. I wanted to see what it was like. I got used to it, got used to the “lifestyle” if you can call it that and now I don’t know anything else.

If only I had kept at it a bit longer. If only I had persevered.

If I could go back to any point in time, it would be to when I was young, specifically to when I graduated from junior high school. At that time, I started working at a bakery. I worked there for about 5 and a half years. We would make all kinds of bread, from plain to sweet bread and then French bread too. Out of what we made, my personal favorites were curry pan and melon pan. Then there's also bread with raisins and stuff like that which I loved. I was in charge of transferring the dough from the conveyor belt onto the iron plate, shape it and then put the dough in the oven to bake. When I started out, I couldn't knead the dough very well. Then I gradually learnt. And when I got used to it, I quit. If only I had kept at it a bit longer. If only I had persevered. I don't know if I would have ended up homeless like this. I was young and doing very early morning shifts though. It was difficult to commute from home in Mitaka to the bakery in Musashisakai… But yeah that was more than 40 years ago.

The hardest thing about being homeless now is food, you know, eating. One thing that I miss is cooking. I used to cook when I was younger. And I miss that.

Clothes are also a big one. I’ll take any winter clothes donation. They’re very helpful, like long sleeves tops. Because it gets colder and colder every day. I keep a close eye on my clothes, bags, rucksacks and things like that. I hope I can prevent them from being stolen.

Work has been harder to come by recently too so I don't really have any money coming in. That's why I'm accepting food from patrols like Tokyo Spring and soup kitchens. In the past, there were a bunch of jobs available in Kawasaki and Yokohama, even Takadanobaba. Now there aren't many jobs available... When I talk about jobs, I mean day jobs. The kind that pays 7,000 yen for 8 hours. In the past, there were quite a lot of such jobs but it’s decreased with the pandemic. It's harder and harder to make money. My money has really been decreasing continuously.

I tried to apply for unemployment benefits once and went to the Kita Ward, near Oji. But I didn’t go through with it. I'm not thinking about it at the moment either. For now, I can be self-reliant and independent. If I lose my job or my own legs and back deteriorate and weaken a bit more, then I'll think about it again and get on welfare. It's quite embarrassing to apply for it and there’s a stigma that comes with receiving it so many people don’t apply.

There are shelters too, dormitories. I was myself in a shelter in Yamaya in the Sanya district. I lived on the third floor, on about three tatami mats (translator's note: around five square meters). I have good memories of that time. Now, from what I hear about, it seems like everyone gets in and out quickly. The turnover there is quite high. I don't know if they all leave because they don't like it. Around the west exit of Shinjuku, where we are now, homeless people come and go. You see someone, then you stop seeing that person for a while. And then they’re back.

I’m mostly invisible to the “regular” people but when they see me they think I'm younger than I am even though I’m 63. They wonder why I'm homeless when I'm so young. And why I don’t work... Even though I do work or try to find work when I don't work.

But I would love if one day I could go to the cinema again.

There is nothing I want now except working so I can get food and winter clothes. But I would love if one day I could go to the cinema again. It would be fun to have such an opportunity again. That or going to the onsen with my friends too if I got the chance. We get our hair cut at the church and it feels good to be clean but the onsen would be nice.

— END OF STORY —
